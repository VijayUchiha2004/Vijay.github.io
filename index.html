BharatCare

  • Random Forest classifier trained on 518 symptom-disease mappings to predict likely conditions from reported symptoms
  • Rule-based environmental risk engine combining real-time weather, AQI, season, and UV index (OpenWeather API) with the ML prediction

Read the case study

Problem

Regional healthcare gaps mean disease risk often isn't visible until symptoms are already advanced, and environmental risk factors specific to a region rarely feed into that picture at all.

Approach

A Random Forest classifier trained on a curated dataset of 518 symptom-disease mappings predicts likely conditions from reported symptoms. A rule-based engine layers in real-time weather, AQI, season, and UV index data from the OpenWeather API to flag context-specific environmental risk. Groq LLaMA 3.1 turns both outputs into plain-language explanations, translated across 10 Indian languages via LibreTranslate.

Stack

React + TypeScript on the frontend, a Flask REST API backend, Scikit-learn for the classifier, Groq LLaMA 3.1 for explanations, and Firebase for authentication and prediction history.

Outcome

The approach was written up and published as a research paper — "BharatCare: An AI-Powered Regional Healthcare Assistant using Machine Learning and Environmental Risk Analysis" — in the International Research Journal of Engineering and Technology (IRJET), Volume 13, Issue 3, March 2026.
